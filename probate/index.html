Automation earns the throughput

Cases per paralegal rise from 50 to 75 — a 50% gain worth roughly five paralegals, ~$475,000 a year, at Year-5 volume. It comes from document assembly, a client document portal, statutory deadline automation in Clio, and intake auto-routing.

Delegation is lawful, and the fee is fixed

B&P § 6450 permits paralegals to draft, research and interview under attorney supervision. Probate Code § 10810 fixes the fee by estate value, not hours — so every hour moved to a supervised paralegal is retained margin. No other practice line has this shape.

Probate Code § 10810

The fee is set by statute

Ordinary attorney compensation is a percentage of the gross estate — identical whether the file takes sixty attorney hours or fifteen attorney hours plus forty-five paralegal hours. On a $500,000 estate, the median reported by the firm's own leads:

First $100,0004%$4,000
Next $100,0003%$3,000
Next $300,0002%$6,000
Attorney fee on a $500,000 estate$13,000

The personal representative earns the same amount separately; this plan counts only the attorney half. Extraordinary fees under § 10811 — real property sales, tax matters, contested issues — are billable on top, but are discretionary and hourly, so they are modelled conservatively at 30% of cases × 20% of the ordinary fee.

Preconditions

What has to be true

Three of these are load-bearing. The plan does not function without them, and none is expensive.

Estate value captured on every lead, at intake

The entire model is a routing decision made before a case is accepted. It is recorded on 104 of 1,000 leads today. A probate fee calculator on the website solves this and the organic-search problem in one build.

A receiving network of 15–30 firms

517 placements in Year 5 is a standing operational function, not a favour. The firm refers to 11 firms today and does so sporadically — 18 referrals have sat unassigned for a median of 168 days.

§ 10830 interim petitions filed as standard

An allowance on account may be sought any time after four months from issuance of Letters. It pulls 45% of every fee forward by roughly six months and carries $3.98M of Year-5 revenue. Without it the cash curve reverts to a far worse shape.

Completion rate — the largest uncertainty

Modelled at 65% of signed cases reaching a statutory fee. Break-even is 38–48%, so there is real margin of safety — but the figure is estimated, not observed. The first true reading arrives as the 2026 cohort matures, around Q2 2027.

Rule 1.5.1 compliance at volume

Every referral needs a written agreement between the lawyers and written client consent after full disclosure. At hundreds a year that is a templated, tracked workflow — non-compliance makes the fee unenforceable. Note also that B&P § 6152 makes paying a non-lawyer per case a misdemeanor, which forecloses lead-vendor and finder's-fee arrangements entirely.

Whether the volume exists

Year-5 volume of 1,100 signed cases is roughly 2.6% of all California probate filings (41,985 in FY2024–25). The measured search market supports the attempt — 12,000–15,000 distinct high-intent monthly searches at low competition — but Year 5 is a target, not a forecast.

In summary

What this plan is

Corcoran Smith Law already originates more probate work than it can deliver. The firm signs the case, collects the $999 assessment, and then refers much of it away for a fraction of its value — or lets it lapse. Probate administration collects $2,125 per matter against litigation’s $913, yet the entire advertising budget points at litigation, and only seven of 276 probate matters have ever collected a statutory-scale fee.

This plan keeps the estates worth keeping, places the rest for a fee, and grows the delivery capacity in step with demand rather than ahead of it.

The model

Capacity sets the line

Retain the top 53% of estates by fee — a $28,556 mean statutory fee — and refer everything below it for a 25% split. Retention never exceeds what the paralegal team can carry, so no case is taken that cannot be delivered.

Why it self-funds

The referred half pays first

A referred case brings $2,500 at intake, costs about $300 to place, and uses no capacity. That cash covers the build while retained cases mature toward their statutory fees 15 to 20 months out.

Why the margin holds

The fee is fixed, the cost is not

Probate Code § 10810 sets compensation by estate value, not hours. Every hour shifted to a supervised paralegal under B&P § 6450 is retained margin — and automation lifts throughput from 50 cases per paralegal to 75.

What it costs to start

Three hires and a budget setting

Year one is two paralegals and an intake coordinator. The existing three attorneys supervise 100 files. The cheapest lead in the firm — Local Services Ads at $21.69, already statewide — is throttled by a $89.29 daily cap.

Where it lands

$11.8M by year five

Profitable in every year, peaking at $7.0M net on 1,100 signed cases, with a probate team of 29 alongside the existing practice — which this model deliberately holds flat at $700,000 rather than crediting it with growth.

What would break it

One number, not yet observed

The plan assumes 65% of signed cases reach a statutory fee. Break-even is 38–48%, so the margin of safety is real — but the figure is estimated. Capturing estate value at intake replaces it with fact, and the first true reading arrives around Q2 2027.
